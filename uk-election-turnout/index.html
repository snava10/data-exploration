05 / DENOMINATOR

The register is not a clean count of everyone eligible

The Electoral Commission measures whether eligible people are correctly registered at their current address.

Electoral register completeness, Great Britain

DECEMBER 2022 REGISTERS
86% correctly registeredEligible people on the register at their current address.
14% not correctly registeredEquivalent to as many as 7–8 million people across the UK not correctly registered at their current address. This includes unregistered people and some registered at an old or incorrect address; estimates carry margins of error.

Denominator warning: general-election turnout divides votes by register entries, not a matched estimate of everyone eligible. Some eligible people are absent, while others may be registered at an old address; the 7–8 million estimate cannot simply be subtracted from the headline denominator.

Source: Electoral Commission, 2023 report on electoral registers.

Incorrect registration is not random: age and time at an address are the strongest predictors, with younger people and private renters least likely to be correctly registered.

A NOTE ON MEASUREMENT

What counts as turnout?

  • 59.7% is the clean historical comparison: valid votes divided by the registered electorate, the House of Commons Library measure used in this article.
  • 60.0% is also published for 2024: the Electoral Commission’s headline includes total ballots cast, including rejected papers. Its voter-ID analysis reports 59.8%. The difference is definitional, not an error.
  • Registered is not the same as eligible: register completeness and accuracy are imperfect. A separate eligible-population turnout needs a date- and geography-aligned denominator; it cannot be derived by treating all 7–8 million incorrectly registered people as wholly absent.
  • Age is survey evidence: ballots are secret and official results do not record voters’ ages. Self-reports tend to overstate voting.
  • Franchises differ: eligibility rules vary across Westminster, devolved and local elections, including for 16–17-year-olds and EU citizens.
